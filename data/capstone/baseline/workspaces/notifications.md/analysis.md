## Confirmed Facts
- The system must send notifications by email.
- The system must send notifications in the app.
- Users must be able to disable ordinary email notifications.
- Urgent notifications must arrive quickly.
- Related notifications must be grouped sensibly.
- Unread in-app notifications must be retained for 30 days.

## Ambiguities
- “ordinary email notifications” does not define which notifications are ordinary, whether users can disable them individually or globally, or whether urgent emails are exempt.
- “must arrive quickly” does not specify a delivery-time target, measurement point, retry policy, or acceptable failure behavior.
- “Group related notifications sensibly” does not define what makes notifications related, the grouping window, grouping presentation, or whether grouping applies to email, in-app notifications, or both.
- “Retain unread in-app notifications for 30 days” does not clarify whether read notifications are deleted immediately, whether the 30 days starts at creation or last update, or what happens after 30 days.
- “Send notifications by email and in the app” does not specify which events trigger notifications, whether every notification must use both channels, or whether users can configure channel preferences.

## Clarification Questions
- Which notification types or events are in scope, and which are classified as ordinary versus urgent?
- Can users disable ordinary email notifications globally, per notification type, or per event? Are urgent emails always enabled?
- What delivery-time SLA defines “quickly” for urgent notifications, and how should retries and delivery failures be handled?
- What criteria and time window determine that notifications are related, and how should grouped notifications appear in email and in-app views?
- Must each notification be delivered through both email and in-app channels, or can channel preferences vary by type?
- How long should read in-app notifications be retained, and should unread notifications expire exactly 30 days after creation? What should users see after expiration?
- Are there privacy, localization, accessibility, or compliance requirements for notification content and delivery?

## Assumptions
- Notifications are generated by defined system events, although those events are not yet enumerated.
- Both email and in-app delivery are required by default unless a user preference or notification classification says otherwise.
- “Urgent” notifications cannot be disabled through the ordinary email setting, pending confirmation.
- The 30-day retention period begins when an in-app notification is created and expired notifications are removed or hidden afterward.
- Grouping is intended to reduce notification volume without losing the underlying related information.

## Summary
The requirement establishes email and in-app notification channels, user control over ordinary email, expedited handling for urgent items, grouping, and 30-day retention for unread in-app notifications. Delivery SLAs, notification classifications, grouping rules, channel preferences, and retention behavior require decisions before implementation.
